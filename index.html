How it started

OmniDocZ wasn't planned as a big project. Its very first "version" was a tiny script to invert the colors of a document—someone asked me for help with a file stuck on a black background (one of those weird dark-mode phone glitches). Thinking they might ask again, I wrapped it in a quick Tkinter window. They never asked again.

For a while, that script just sat there. Then I remembered a frustrating moment from before I started studying Software Engineering: I needed to fill out and submit a form, had no printer ink to print, write, and scan it, so I uploaded a document full of sensitive information to a web tool. Editing it was painful, and when I finally finished and hit export, it demanded payment. Back then, I didn't have the experience to realize solving it myself wasn't impossible.

With that script already open, I started adding the small things I actually needed in daily life. First came a file-size limiter (after struggling with abusive websites just to compress a photo under 2 MB). Then I remembered that form and built a basic editor that simply placed text on top of a page—back then, that alone would have saved me.

Little by little, I realized how many everyday document problems are already solved, yet people are forced onto sketchy websites—handing over private files and often hitting a paywall at the very last step. That's where NightmareZProtocol was born: unified, honest desktop solutions for ordinary problems.

The NightmareZProtocol Manifesto

NightmareZProtocol can be summed up in one idea: being the exact opposite of freemium software.

Private and offline by default

Your documents, IDs, forms, and photos never need to touch a stranger's server just to be merged, compressed, edited, or OCR'd. Processing happens on your own PC.

Free, unlimited, no dark patterns

No surprise paywalls at export, no "3 files per day" counters, no accounts, and no ads. As a student, I'd rather build useful tools and contribute than lock basics behind subscriptions.

No app born English-only

Spanish speakers—and everyday users or older adults around the world—are constantly overlooked by software. Every tool I build ships with multi-language support from the start.

Built for real daily use

I still use OmniDocZ myself every day, which forces me to hold a high standard for reliability. (It also explains why Excel mode is still in beta—I've honestly never used Excel!)

Honest engineering & what's next

Because OmniDocZ grew organically out of a quick Tkinter script—unlike later projects such as MetashieldZ, which was designed for public distribution from day one—it kept a classic, utilitarian look. Functionality has always come first for me, though migrating the interface to PySide6 is on the table once the core reaches the level I want, depending on community support.

Building OmniDocZ as my first public GitHub project—with its own updater, offline AI engine, and multi-format workflow—has taught me far more than coursework alone ever could. Right now, my focus is making OmniDocZ the solid heart of the NightmareZProtocol ecosystem: free, private, and unlimited, today and going forward.
